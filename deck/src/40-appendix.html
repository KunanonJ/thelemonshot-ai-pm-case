Appendix A2 · Proposal Copilot (PC-01)

The first release: a simpler version of the same pattern

Brief form or Slack→ Claude parses the brief
JSON schema→ SQL: top 10 past case studies
by category and objective→ Claude drafts slide-by-slide JSON
with 3 Big Idea territories→ Drive files.copy
master template→ Slides batchUpdate
replaceAllText→ Number-grounding check→ Slack to the strategist
AI-DRAFT watermark→ Log the AI content survival rate
Why no vector database in v1

With a 1M-token context, SQL filtering plus the full text of the top 10 case studies is enough. Add BigQuery VECTOR_SEARCH with gemini-embedding-001 (supports Thai) when the library outgrows that.

Guardrails

Every figure in the deck must match a source row, or it is highlighted for review. The Big Idea is strategist-owned: AI offers 3 territories, never a final answer. Nothing goes to a client from the workflow.

Endpoints

POST googleapis.com/drive/v3/files/{id}/copy
POST slides.googleapis.com/v1/presentations/{id}:batchUpdate with replaceAllText and replaceAllShapesWithImage.

Success for v1: a first draft in under a day instead of about three, and strategist edits concentrated on the Big Idea instead of formatting and research.
Proposal Copilot ships first because it needs documents, not a data mart. Same skeleton as Creator Match: form, LLM with a schema, Google output, Slack approval, logging.
Appendix A3 · Campaign Pulse (CP-01)

Recommend, approve, act, then learn from the result

Daily at 08:00→ BigQuery
yesterday's ads, creator posts, sales→ Rules vs targets
CPA, CPV, ER vs benchmark, fatigue→ Claude explains
up to 3 actions, impact, confidence→ Slack approval
to the planner→ If approved and in guardrails
Meta POST /v26.0/{adset_id}→ 72 h later
did it work?→ Outcomes to the creator graph
Data in

Meta GET /v26.0/{adset_id}/insights?fields=spend,impressions,clicks,actions&date_preset=yesterday&level=ad, TikTok API for Business reports, creator post metrics, and promo-code and affiliate sales. The sales data is the RMN seed.

Guardrails

At most ±20% budget change a day, a 24 h cooldown, one-click revert, restricted approvers. Budgets are sent in minor units (THB offset 100). Nothing changes without a named approval.

Why it matters

Real outcomes turn Creator Match from "looks right" into "proven to sell", and they become case studies and benchmarks for Proposal Copilot.

"Automated adjustments" in v1 means automated recommendations. The machine prepares the change; a planner makes it. We loosen this only when the outcome data earns it.
This is how I'd read "automated adjustments" responsibly: AI prepares, the planner approves, and every action is evaluated 72 hours later so we learn which recommendations to trust.

Appendix A5 · Creator Match scoring

A transparent formula, with starting weights to calibrate

match = wperf x Performance + waud x Audience fit + wbrand x Brand fit + wcost x Cost efficiency  (each 0-100)
Components
  • Performance: 70% campaign history vs benchmark, Bayesian-shrunk (k = 3) so one lucky campaign doesn't dominate, plus 30% engagement vs tier median.
  • Audience fit: the share of the creator's audience matching the brief's age, gender and location, from consented demographics or a vendor.
  • Brand fit: Claude's 0-100 judgement from recent captions, tags and comment sentiment, citing posts. If it can't be assessed, audience fit stands in and the row is flagged "not assessed".
  • Cost efficiency: the creator's cost per expected engagement compared with the category benchmark.
Starting weights by objective
ObjectivePerf.Aud.BrandCost

Rules around the score: hard rules first (consent, blocked, competitor work in 90 days, rate cap of 1.2x budget per creator). Then budget fit, at least one rising creator, and flags shown, never dropped. Weights are calibrated with CDs in the pilot, then against campaign outcomes.

If asked why these weights: sales briefs reward proven conversion, awareness rewards reaching the right audience, and community briefs live or die on tone. They are starting points. The pilot calibrates them with CDs, then campaign outcomes calibrate them for real.
Appendix A6 · Likely questions

Short answers to the hard questions

"RMN is our strategy. Why not first?"It's a data product that needs a clean creator graph, commerce attribution, consent and engineers. Phase 1 produces the first three as by-products, so Year 2 starts well ahead instead of from zero.
"How do you get audience data without scraping?"Public metrics via Business Discovery. Demographics via creator OAuth through Creator House, in exchange for insights. A vendor or TikTok One for the rest. Never scraping.
"What if Meta or TikTok change their APIs?"External data lands in BigQuery through one refresh workflow per platform, and scoring reads only from the marts. Pin versions, watch changelogs, keep a vendor fallback.
"Won't it always pick the same big creators?"Tier normalization, Bayesian shrinkage, a rising-creator rule, cost per engagement instead of follower count, and monitoring of how picks spread across tiers.
"How do you know the LLM is any good?"A golden set of 30-50 past briefs: overlap with expert picks, brand-safety recall, rationale faithfulness, JSON validity. It gates every prompt or model change.
"What if the analysts get pulled into reporting?"Campaign Pulse automates part of reporting to buy their time back, and the 70% allocation is my first ask.
"Isn't no-code a bus-factor risk?"Workflows exported to Git, naming conventions, a runbook, analysts cross-trained, and OPS-01 monitoring with alerts.
"What if the CDs refuse?"No mandate. Volunteers first, shadow mode, take away the work they hate, let peers show the results, and respect the off switch.
