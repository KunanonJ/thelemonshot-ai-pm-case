Task 3 · Risks I must control

Three risks to control before any third-party gen AI touches client work

01

Creator likeness and personality rights

Risk

A vendor tool reproduces a creator's face, voice or style beyond what they agreed to, or trains on creator assets. Our celebrity network raises the stakes.

Controls
  • AI Likeness Rider: opt-in per use, payment for synthetic variants, approval rights, a kill switch, no vendor training.
  • Consent registry checked in code before any generative step that touches creator assets.
  • Platform labels: TikTok, YouTube and Meta AI-disclosure rules.
02

Synthetic media licensing and output IP

Risk

Clients receive AI-generated assets whose ownership is unclear, or that infringe third-party IP through the vendor's training data.

Controls
  • Vendor contract: output ownership to us or the client, IP indemnity, a warranty that training data is licensed.
  • Asset register: model, prompt, human edits and approvals, plus C2PA credentials where supported.
  • Client contracts disclose AI use and allocate risk.
03

Training-data privacy and client confidentiality

Risk

A vendor retains or trains on briefs, personal data or results. With 750+ brands, data could leak between competing clients through shared retrieval.

Controls
  • Contract terms: no training, limited retention, a DPA, PDPA cross-border safeguards.
  • Minimize: send aggregates, redact personal data, and isolate each client in retrieval.
  • Operate: role-based access, audit logs, a DPIA before go-live.
Principle: consent and confidentiality are enforced in code, not in a policy document. If the registry says no, the workflow cannot run.
These must be in place before any third-party generative platform enters client work, which is also why DCO waits. The most agency-specific risk is number three: we serve competing brands, so a shared AI memory could leak one client's strategy into another's proposal. Retrieval is isolated per client, by design.

Listen first

Shadow 8 users across strategy, creative, media and talent. Run the time study. Form a design council that includes the most respected skeptical CD. Co-write the "AI must never..." list.

Months 1-2 · Shadow pilot

Prove it safely

One squad on social-commerce clients, where outcomes are measurable. AI runs alongside the manual process. Nothing reaches a client until the squad says it's useful.

Months 2-4 · Champions

Let users sell it

One champion per squad. A weekly 15-minute show-and-tell run by users, not by me. "AI Clinic" office hours, borrowing the Creator Clinic idea. A public "you said, we changed" log.

Months 4-6 · Scale

Make it the default

Squad-by-squad onboarding: 30 minutes plus a playbook. Leaders use the tools in pitch reviews. AI-assisted wins are recognized at town halls. Default on, still optional.

Messaging

"Less admin, more ideas." Never framed as headcount reduction. Leadership says out loud that time saved is reinvested in creative work.

Incentives

Credit goes to the strategist on every win. People who give feedback and labels are recognized. AI use shows up in reviews as craft, not as compliance.

Change management is product work. Start with the work people hate, prove it on their own briefs in shadow mode, and let users, not me, do the demos. The skeptical CD on the design council is deliberate: if they help write the rules, the floor trusts the rules.
Task 4 · Human-in-the-loop design

Empowerment by design: guardrails, overrides, feedback

GProduct guardrails

  • Evidence or silence. Every score shows its breakdown, sources and data date. Thin data gets "low confidence", never a guess.
  • Hard rules run before the AI. Exclusions, consent, blocked creators and budget caps are code the LLM cannot override.
  • No autonomous outward actions. Clients, creators and spend each need a named approval, with an audit trail.
  • Spend caps in Pulse: at most ±20% a day, a 24 h cooldown, one-click revert.
  • AI-DRAFT watermark until a human marks each section reviewed.

OCreative override controls

  • Swap, remove, add or reorder any creator. Edit anything. Regenerate a section with an instruction.
  • Human-owned zones: the Big Idea and creative territories belong to the strategist. AI offers options only on request.
  • Tune the matrix per brief with presets like "proven sales" or "rising creators".
  • One-click override reasons. Never penalized. The human choice is the decision of record.
  • An off switch per brief for any squad lead.

FActive feedback loops

  • Inline keep or remove with a reason, and a rating on each rationale.
  • Weekly prompt and weight updates, published as "you said, we changed".
  • Outcome calibration: predicted vs actual for AI-picked and human-added creators. Used to learn, never to grade people.
  • A monthly AI Council with CDs reviews edge cases and approves weight changes.
  • A golden set of 30-50 past briefs gates every prompt or model change.
The AI earns trust by being checkable, overridable and visibly improving. A Creative Director should feel like the editor, never the reviewer of a machine.
Three layers. Guardrails the AI can't break, because they're code, not prompts. Overrides that are one click and never penalized, so disagreeing with the AI is cheap. Feedback loops that visibly change the product every week, which is what turns skeptics into contributors.
